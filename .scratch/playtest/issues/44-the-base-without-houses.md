## Report

#42 took the base out of the age climb, and #43 takes the House out of the base: the room for
villagers is now **housing**, a route resource. What is left of the base is a Town Center standing
or paid for by the Town Center items, the Villager Line, Starting Villager Food, and the scenario's
ground (`meets_additional_base_requirements`, such as Joan 3's crossing).

`has_base()` is asked by local start, farming, and the at-scale villager routes ("Lumberjacks with
a Base", villagers gathering food, gold and stone, and the villager-and-boat food and gold routes).

## Open for triage

- Does the base stay, slimmed to "P1 can train villagers": a Town Center, the Villager Line and the
  food for the first ones?
- Does the scenario's ground stay in the base, or move to the at-scale routes that work across the
  water? #42 already ruled a Town Center stands on home ground.
- Or does the base go, with each caller asking its parts directly? Local start still aims for a base
  (ADR-0003).
- Does it keep the name "base"?

**Category:** enhancement

**Summary:** The base stays, under its name, slimmed to one rule. Its Town Center half becomes the
`has_building(TOWN_CENTER)` it was spelling out longhand, `can_build_base` and `can_have_base` go,
and local start asks the named seed items instead of resource amounts.

**Current behavior**

`has_base()` is a `Named` over `can_have_base()` and three further asks, and `can_have_base()` is
in turn an `Or` of a standing Town Center against `can_build_base()`, which since #43 is a bare
alias returning `can_build_tc()`. Written out, the Town Center half is:

    standing | (Town Center building item & builders & TOWN_CENTER_WOOD & TOWN_CENTER_STONE)

which is what `has_building(TOWN_CENTER)` already answers, bar a `has_reached(DARK)` no scenario is
below. The longhand copy is rebuilt at every call site; `ScenarioHasBuilding` is memoised once per
scenario (#42). Three rules name the one concept, and the base is asked at 34 route leaves, by
farming, and by local start.

Local start's `base_target` asks the Town Center's and the villagers' cost as **amounts**
(`has_amounts`, `has_amount`, resolving to `HasResourceAmount`), which any mix of resource items can
meet. Where `has_base()` is live that is masked, because it separately demands the named seed items.
Where it degrades to `True_()` - a fixed force, which is Joan 1, the case local start exists for -
nothing names them, so a Joan 1 start can be handed `+250 Starting Wood` and `+50 Starting Wood` in
place of `Starting Town Center Wood`.

**Desired behavior**

*One base rule.* `has_base()` is the only base rule, and reads as four asks:

- `has_building(TOWN_CENTER)` - standing, or built and paid for with both Town Center items;
- the scenario's ground (`meets_additional_base_requirements`);
- the Villager Line;
- Starting Villager Food.

It stays a plain `Named`, not a scenario question. `can_build_base` and `can_have_base` are deleted.
Nothing else about what the base asks changes: the Villager Line and the villagers' food stay the
base's, and the ground stays the base's.

*Local start.* `base_target` asks `TOWN_CENTER_WOOD`, `TOWN_CENTER_STONE` and
`STARTING_VILLAGER_FOOD` by name in place of the three amount asks. It keeps asking them
unconditionally, so a scenario whose Town Center already stands is still handed one it can put up.
Everything else about the target is unchanged: the Town Center building item, the House where the
civilisation builds one, and `scenario_base_rule` with its `always_false` degradation.

`has_amount`, `has_amounts` and `HasResourceAmount` **stay**. Local start is not their only caller:
`_has_seeded_building` and the `TRADE_SEED_*` banked piles need them, and ADR-0003's banked-pile
consequence rests on them.

*The one player-visible change.* A fixed-force local start receives the seed items sized to what
they buy, rather than generic resource items summing to the same numbers.

**Key interfaces**

- `ScenarioLogic.has_base()`: the single base rule, built on `has_building(TOWN_CENTER)`.
  `can_have_base()` is gone; `ScenarioBuildingLogic.can_build_base()` is gone.
- `LocalStart.base_target`: the Town Center's and villagers' cost named as seed items. Its docstring
  explains itself by contrast with `can_build_base`, which will not exist - contrast with `has_base`
  instead, and say the House it asks is the Houses route's item, not the base's.
- `test_local_start.assert_pays_for_a_town_centre`: asserts the named items rather than resource
  totals.

## Comments

> *This was generated by AI during triage.* Grilling session, 2026-10-28.

**Rulings from the grill.**

- **The base survives, under its name.** Its glossary entry, rewritten under #43, describes the
  post-#44 base without a word changed. A term whose definition survives the change was describing
  the right thing.
- **The first villagers do not follow the House out.** Housing left because it recurs with every
  unit trained; the Villager Line and the villagers' food are a one-time opening cost paid by a seed
  item. This is the one answer here with a real alternative, so it is recorded in ADR-0003.
- **The ground stays.** #42's reversal was scoped to the advance: a Town Center goes up on home
  ground. The at-scale villager routes are the settlement, and the settlement is what has to be
  across the water. Only Joan 3 sets it; moving it out restates one scenario's rule at 34 sites.
- **One rule, not three.** After the Town Center half becomes `has_building`, `can_have_base` would
  be "the paid Town Center and the ground", which its own docstring ("A base, less paying for it")
  would be lying about.
- **No `ScenarioHasBase`.** #42 made questions out of rules instantiated 505,121 times per seed from
  inside every building, tech and unit location. Route lists are built once per scenario behind
  `ScenarioHasEconomyRoutes`, so the base is built about 36 times, and since `has_own_easy_source`
  was dropped (below) no caller builds them unmemoised at all.
- **Local start names its seed items.** Maintainer's ruling. The follow-on "and then the amount
  rules can go" does not hold: `_has_seeded_building` and the `TRADE_SEED_*` piles still use them.

**Why the amount asks were not equivalent.** `HasResourceAmount(WOOD, 275)` is met by any sum of
wood contributors:

| Item | Wood |
|---|---|
| +50 / +100 / +250 Starting Wood | 50, 100, 250 |
| Starting Town Center Wood | 275 |
| Starting Lumber Camp Wood | 100 |
| Starting Mule Cart Resources | 80 |
| Starting Dock Wood | 150 |
| Starting Market Wood | 175 |

`solve` shuffles candidates and trims greedily, so which subset survives is a per-seed coin flip.
Where `has_base()` is live, its `Has(TOWN_CENTER_WOOD)` forces the seed item and the strays trim
away. Where it degrades to `True_()` nothing does, which is why a Joan 1 start changes. The seed
items are sized to what they buy - 275 wood against a 275-wood Town Center, 100 stone against 100,
150 food - and `BuildingSeeds.py` asserts that sizing.

**Evidence shape.** `red_check.py`'s own docstring says a test that still passes at HEAD "guards
unchanged behaviour, or proves nothing". That is what this ticket's tests are for, so the red-check
ritual is dropped deliberately rather than forgotten. Do not reinstate it.

**Dropped during triage (`ac5e1f8a0`).** `has_own_easy_source` had no production caller and never
had one: `d8d294259` added it alongside the two tests that called it, where it read as production
API beside the live `has_easy_source` and `has_some_source`. It is deleted, and the construction it
wrapped now lives in `test/bases.py` as `own_easy_source`, which `test_base_through_price.py` and
`test_economy_rules.py` call.
