## Report

Issue 01's audit listed scenario rules that count on units a trigger hands over: Transport Ships
(Joan 1, Joan 3, Genghis 3, the Joan 2 Dock route), Trade Carts with their escort (Joan 2), and the
French Army (Joan 6). The first ruling was that ships and carts are not cavemanified in game, which
would make those rows harmless. In the playtest, transports arrived in Joan 1 and Trade Carts in
Joan 2 without being knocked down, but both lines may already have been unlocked at the time.

### Triage Notes (2026-10-22)

**What we've established so far:**

- `ApplyCaveman` (`Caveman.xs:207`) leaves a unit alone only when:
  - its type is not in the unit table;
  - its row is `cavemanExempt` (villager lines only, `UnitData.is_caveman_exempt`);
  - it is on the immune list (mercenaries, `MercenarySpawn.xs:77`);
  - its type is exempted with `ExemptUnitTypeFromCaveman`, which only the Flemish Militia trap uses.
    `CavemanExemption()` is empty in the scenarios checked.
- The Transport Ship Line (834) and Trade Cart Line (819) are unit-line locations (`UnitLines.py`).
  With unitsanity on, their rows are in the table, and a row stays `locked` until its items arrive
  (`setUnitHidden`, `Unitsanity.xs:157`).
- A locked row has no unlocked tier, so `cavemanTarget` falls back to `CAVEMAN_MILITIA`. Going by
  the code, a trigger-granted Transport Ship whose line is still locked becomes a Militia on the
  water, or is deleted if that spawn fails. A Trade Cart becomes a Militia. This comes from reading
  code only; it has not been reproduced.
- With unitsanity off, these rows are not in the table, so caveman leaves the units alone.
- The playtest observations so far cannot tell "exempt" apart from "already unlocked".

### Triage Notes (2026-10-28)

**#10 has landed, and it removes one of the three explanations.**

- Ageipelago `fcfc0c0` makes `UnitsanityChecks` `highFrequency` (`Unitsanity.xs:437`), and
  `collectNewUnits` (`:386`) diffs this tick's P1 id list against last tick's **by id**. A unit a
  trigger hands over is swept on the next tick whether or not the unit count moved.
- So "the sweep simply hadn't run yet", the third explanation the 2026-10-27 note raised, is gone.
  What is left is "exempt" against "already unlocked".

**The code path is unchanged, and `transformUnit` is worse than the first ruling assumed.**

- `cavemanTarget` (`Caveman.xs:151`) still falls back to `CAVEMAN_MILITIA` (74) when no tier of the
  line is unlocked, and `technologyUnlocked` (`:136`) is false for any `locked` row. The Transport
  Ship (line 834) and the Trade Cart (line 819) are **single-tier lines**: there is nothing below
  them to fall back to, so a locked row goes straight to Militia.

### Ruling (2026-10-28)

**Yes, they are knocked down.** [#46](46-caveman-catalog-spike.md) run 2 probed all 107 rows of
the unit table on land and on a real water tile (terrain 23 at (96, 96)), ledger 19206 of 19206:

- All 17 water rows swapped to a Militia **created on the water tile itself**. Nothing was
  destroyed — `transformUnit`'s `xsCreateUnit` never failed, on either spot.
- Every non-exempt row targets Militia at a fully locked table. The 40 `cavemanExempt` rows held.
- **No ship is exempt**, and no cart is. Heroes and villagers are, through `owned_type_row`
  (`client/handlers/install/UnitData.py:130`), which writes `exempt=True` without consulting
  `is_caveman_exempt`.

**And the Militia is stuck.** Per the maintainer, from play: a Militia left on open water cannot
move off it, **and that is acceptable** — no game-side change. So a scenario-granted ship under
caveman leaves the player nothing usable until the line's items arrive.

That is the first branch of the 2026-10-22 decision tree: **a logic bug**. The rows below ask for
a granted unit and get a stranded Militia, so they must ask whether the unit survives caveman.

**Out of scope, decided here:** no XS change. `UnitData.is_caveman_exempt` is not widened, ships
and carts stay cavemanifiable, and `transformUnit` keeps its remove-then-create order.

Two things this makes plain that #16 and #01 both missed:

- **Startup units are in scope.** Joan 5 opens with 10 units, 7 of which caveman turns into

#### What is already correct, verified

Most of that table turns out not to matter, and the audit should say so rather than re-check it
per scenario:

- **Unit locations are gated.** `UnitRules.location_rule` (`rules/UnitRules.py:53`) sends them to
  `can_own_anywhere`, which is `can_train_anywhere | (is_granted_anywhere & survives_caveman) |
  can_convert` (`logic/unit_logic.py:36`). A granted unit that caveman takes away does not satisfy
  its own location.
- **The military checks never count a granted unit.** `has_military`, `has_siege`,
  `has_long_range_siege`, `has_navy`, `can_guard_boats`, `counters`, `counters_building` and
  `can_hold_open_ground` all reduce through `ScenarioUnitLogic._field`, which is built from
  `can_train_structurally` alone. #01 asserted this; it is now read and confirmed.
- **Heroes and escort units are caveman-exempt**, so `is_granted_anywhere` is the right rule for
  them with no gate, which is what `location_rule` does.
- **Villager lines are exempt**, so `has_vils` and the villager job rows are safe wherever the
  grant is a villager.
- **Buildings are not cavemanified**, so `starts_with_building` is safe.

#### The seam to watch, not yet a finding

`UnitRules.entrance_rule` (`:65`) opens a unit region's **startup** entrance with `True_()` and
its **trigger** entrance with a bare `obtains_unit(grant)` — neither asks `survives_caveman`.
That does not leak today, because the locations inside the region carry their own gated rule and
the region holds nothing else. It is worth a note so nobody later moves a requirement onto the
entrance and loses the gate silently.

#### So what the audit is actually looking for

Per scenario, the rows where a **location rule names a granted unit or its item directly**,
rather than going through `can_own_anywhere` or a military check. Plus the scenario facts built
from grants: `has_vils`, `must_steal_base`, `meets_additional_base_requirements`, and anything in
`defeat_rules` or the scenario's own `*Rules.set_rules` that asks `Has(<grant item>)`.
