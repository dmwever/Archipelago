## Report

> *This was generated by AI during triage.*

Once #05 lands, age reach used as a prerequisite (`has_reached`) stays structural, so it no longer
pulls in a base. Partner trade by Trade Cog then needs no base at all:

- `can_run_trade_cogs`: a trade partner's dock, Feudal (structural), a Dock standing or buildable,
  Trade Cog items, and wood that is chopped **or banked** (`TRADE_SEED_WOOD_AT_SEA`).
- `_can_seed_trade_gold`: `can_gather_gold()` (relics, a little mining, oysters, whales, none asking
  a base) **or** banked gold (`TRADE_SEED_GOLD`).

With banked wood and gold, cogs are an easy wood and easy gold source without a base, and through
easy wood also feed endless food and market trade. Trade carts still ask `can_gather_wood()`, which
asks a base.

## Context

Not necessarily a bug. The maintainer's direction (2026-10-22) is to **decouple the base from being
the only way to have an economy**: locations reach the base only through their price, and later work
adds non-base routes to the easy sources. Cog trade may be one of those routes, kept on purpose.

### Triage Notes (2026-10-22)

The maintainer widened this ticket: the base is too specific to be the economy (it asks a Town
Center, Houses, villagers), so the question became what the base pays for and what could seed each
resource instead. Audit (after #05 lands): the base gates easy wood, easy gold, easy stone and raw
easy food directly, and through `can_gather_wood()` it gates endless food, market trade, Trade Carts
and Fishing Ships. Only Trade Cogs (banked wood and gold, or `can_gather_gold()`) and scenario
`easy_resource_sources` reach an easy source with no base.

Decisions from grilling, recorded in `CONTEXT.md` (**Economy route**, **Easy source**, **Economy
seed**, **Base**) and in [ADR-0003](../../../worlds/age2de/docs/adr/0003-economy-is-routes-resolved-together.md):

- The economy is a set of **economy routes**: a producer, its yields, a strength, and an **economy
  seed** (the bundle it needs before it yields). The base is the economy seed of villagers gathering
  at scale.
- Two strengths: **some** (pays an economy seed once) and **at scale** (keeps paying). Only an easy
  source, a route at scale, pays a location's price (ADR-0002).
- An economy seed's resources may come from **any** route at either strength. Routes therefore ask
  one another in cycles, which `ScenarioQuestion` refuses (`RecursionError`), so a scenario's routes
  are resolved together from the state, to a fixpoint.
- Villager count never matters. Banks are *some* only above each economy seed's authored amount.
  Relics yield only *some* gold. Local start still aims for a base. Scenarios keep
  `easy_resource_sources` (at scale) and gain a hook for *some*.
- The cog is the only route an economy seed of *some* can start; every other at-scale route needs a
  base or wood at scale.
- Split in two: this ticket lands the mechanism with today's catalog, **changing no answer**; the
  catalog changes are [#22](22-economy-route-catalog.md).

## Agent Brief

> *This was generated by AI during triage.* Approved 2026-10-22 after grilling.

**Category:** enhancement
**Summary:** Resolve a scenario's easy sources as a set of economy routes worked out together, with every answer unchanged

**Current behavior:**
Each easy source (`ScenarioHasEasyResource`, behind `ScenarioResourceLogic.has_easy_source` /
`can_sustain` / `can_pay`) is a tree of plain rules that ask one another: easy wood asks chopping or
cog trade, cog trade asks chopping or a bank, market trade asks easy wood or endless food, and so on.
Those trees must stay acyclic, because `ScenarioQuestion.answered` raises `RecursionError` on a
question that asks itself; the code avoids cycles by hand (see the "leaf gatherers only" comment on
market trade). That rules out what ADR-0003 needs: one route's yield seeding another, cycles
included.

**Desired behavior:**
- A scenario's economy is declared as **economy routes** (glossary terms; use them in names,
  docstrings and test names). Each route has a name, the resources it yields, a strength per resource
  (*some* or *at scale*), and an **economy seed** in two parts:
  - a **leaf part**: a plain rule that asks no easy source and no other route (items, buildings
    structurally, access, villagers, the job, the map's resources, tier gates, banks);
  - **route needs**: a set of (resource, strength) pairs, met by any running route yielding that
    resource at that strength or stronger (*at scale* satisfies *some*).
- One resolver per scenario evaluates the routes against a `CollectionState` to a least fixpoint:
  start with no route running; repeatedly switch on every route whose leaf part holds and whose route
  needs the running routes meet; stop when nothing changes. A route never seeds itself into running
  (a cog's own wood cannot start the cog).
- `has_easy_source(resource)` holds exactly when a running route yields `resource` at scale. A
  sibling question answers *some*.
- **Today's catalog, unchanged.** Every route is declared so that every easy-source answer, in every
  state, is identical to the current code (after #05). Where today's rules name a specific gatherer
  (for example Trade Carts and Fishing Ships asking `can_gather_wood()`, i.e. villagers chopping with
  a base, and not cog wood), that stays in the leaf part so the answer does not widen. Moving those to
  route needs is #22.
- **The naval guard is a route need, not leaf** (folded in from
  [#21](21-structural-field-asks-price.md)). The `"afloat"` tier gate (deep fish, whales) and the
  `"shoreline"` gate (shore fish, oysters) ask `can_protect_boats`, whose navy today asks
  `economy.can_sustain(...)`: easy sources inside what must be leaf. Split it: the leaf part keeps
  the navy fielded structurally (`can_field_structurally` with no `can_sustain`) and water access;
  the navy's cost (wood and gold for the shipped lines) becomes route needs **at scale** of every
  route working through those gates. The shoreline gate is `can_hold_a_shoreline() | guard`; an Or
  between a leaf way and a way with route needs may take one route per way. Answers stay identical
  on every shipped scenario, and the oyster/whale loop in Barbarossa and Saladin (easy gold ->
  guard -> easy gold) resolves instead of raising `RecursionError`.
- Scenarios keep `easy_resource_sources` as an at-scale scenario route, and gain a sibling
  `ScenarioStartingState` field for scenario routes yielding *some* (default: nothing for every
  resource). No scenario sets it in this ticket.
- The resolver evaluates once per state change, not once per question: the answer for all resources
  is shared and cached the way rule_builder caches other rules, and its item dependencies are the
  union of every route's leaf part, so cache invalidation stays correct.
- The logic explanation (`/explain`) still reads one line per easy source, and can name which routes
  were running.

**Key interfaces:**
- `ScenarioHasEasyResource`: answered from the resolver rather than its own rule tree. Its key and
  description are unchanged.
- `ScenarioResourceLogic.has_easy_source`, `can_sustain`, `can_pay`: same signatures and meaning.
- A route declaration type and a resolver `Rule` (with its `Resolved`); names are the agent's choice
  but use **Economy route** / **Economy seed**.
- `ScenarioStartingState`: a new `dict[Resource, Rule]` field for scenario routes of *some*,
  alongside `easy_resource_sources`.

## Implementation (2026-10-23)

> *This was generated by AI during implementation.*

- `logic/custom_logic/EconomyRoutes.py`: `EconomyRoute` (name, yields with a strength per
  resource, a `leaf` part and route `needs`), the resolver `EconomyRoutes` (a least fixpoint per
  state, shared by every resource: one resolution per state, its siblings written to the rule
  cache), and `EconomyRoutesYield`. `ScenarioHasEconomyRoutes`, reached through
  `ScenarioResourceLogic.has_economy_routes()`, caches one resolver per scenario.
  `ScenarioHasEasyResource` and the new `ScenarioHasSomeResource` read it.
- The catalog lives in `ScenarioResourceLogic.economy_routes()`: P1's own routes (Lumberjacks,
  Trade Cogs, Trade Carts, Farms and Fish Traps, Food Gatherers, Gold Miners, Stone Miners), Market
  Trade, and the scenario's own (`easy_resource_sources` at scale, the new `some_resource_sources`
  of some).
- The naval guard is a route need. `ScenarioMilitaryLogic.boat_guards()` groups the navy lines by
  cost. Each group adds a "... with Guarded Boats" route whose leaf uses that group's structural
  guard and whose needs are the group's costs at scale. The unguarded route keeps the old answer
  when the guard is unpaid. `tier_gates` takes the guard as a parameter.
- `/explain`: an easy source still reads one line. Opening it lists the routes, each green when
  running and openable into its leaf.

**Criterion 6 (resolved 2026-10-23: the maintainer accepted the Attila 6 test).** Barbarossa and
Saladin are not connected here (no `Age2CampaignData`, no `Age2ScenarioData` members). Their oysters
also sit in `Tier.BASE`, whose gate is `True_`, so even connected they would never ask the guard
and never loop. The test instead puts Barbarossa 3's 1400 oysters in Attila 6's open tier (water, a
fieldable navy), which reproduces the `RecursionError` on HEAD and resolves after.
